Hi. My name is Devashish Tupkary. I recently completed my PhD in Physics (Quantum Information) at the [Institute for Quantum Computing](https://uwaterloo.ca/institute-for-quantum-computing/), University of Waterloo, advised by [Norbert L&uuml;tkenhaus](https://uwaterloo.ca/physics-astronomy/people-profiles/norbert-lutkenhaus), and supported by the Mike and Ophelia Lazaridis Fellowship. During my graduate studies, I primarily worked on the rigorous security analysis of practical quantum key distribution protocols. 

My PhD thesis, [Rigorous Security Proofs for Practical Quantum Key Distribution](https://uwspace.uwaterloo.ca/items/aa7fd015-b9ed-4524-9b53-5794ee66f8c8), is intended to be a self-contained and pedagogical reference for the field — if you want a single place to learn how QKD security proofs are built from the ground up, that may be a good place to start. 


 I am currently a postdoctoral researcher at IQC, and will be joining the [University of Montreal](https://diro.umontreal.ca/english/home/) as a postdoctoral researcher starting September 2026.

Previously, I got my BSc (Research) degree from the [Indian Institute of Science](https://iisc.ac.in/), Bengaluru in 2020. During my undergraduate degree, I worked with [Manas Kulkarni](https://www.icts.res.in/people/manas-kulkarni) and [Abhishek Dhar](https://www.icts.res.in/people/abhishek-dhar) at [ICTS-TIFR](https://www.icts.res.in), on physically consistent descriptions of open quantum systems. 




I also work as a Research Analyst at [evolutionQ](https://www.evolutionq.com), where I work on cryptographic key management for QKD networks and lead consulting engagements and security reviews for client QKD implementations.

# Research Interests
I am broadly interested in both the theoretical and practical aspects of quantum information and computation, with a particular focus on Quantum Key Distribution (QKD). My research centers on rigorous security analysis of practical QKD protocols — closing the gap between idealized security proofs and the imperfect devices, finite statistics, and practical constraints found in real implementations. I am especially engaged with the pathway towards certification, working to make QKD security proofs precise and complete enough to support standardization.

As I move into my postdoc, I'm looking to branch out beyond QKD and engage with broader problems across quantum information and cryptography.

I also continue to do a little work on accurate descriptions of open quantum systems, aimed at understanding the validity and limitations of various quantum master equations, beyond specific system–bath models.
